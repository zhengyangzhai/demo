Audio & Annotation Demo

Selected Q-R pairs from the PQSR benchmark, illustrating how cross-modal consistency and discrepancy shape pragmatic intent in Chinese spoken language.

Task Overview

The PQSR benchmark covers two complementary tasks that share speech-text inputs: PQP (Pragmatic Question Pair) — binary literal vs. deep classification on the question utterance; SR (Speech Act Recognition) — 14-class fine-grained intention recognition on the response utterance. The samples below are drawn from the test set and are grouped as Q-R conversational pairs.

Audio Samples

Pair 1 and Pair 3 share identical question text ("现在下课了吗？" — "Is class over?") but differ in prosody and pragmatic intent. Listen and compare: in Pair 1 the speaker uses a calm, neutral rising tone typical of a genuine information-seeking question (literal); in Pair 3 the speaker uses a sharper, impatient falling contour signaling a rhetorical reprimand (deep) — the speaker already knows the answer and is expressing dissatisfaction.

Annotation Highlights

Each sample ships with a multi-tier Praat TextGrid and a PitchTier file. A single response utterance may contain multiple prosodic phrases, and each phrase is independently labeled with a speech act from the 14-class SR taxonomy (e.g., F = Factual, W = Wisdom/advice, A = Against, D = Disagreement, R = Resonance/agreement, V = Vague). The arrows below indicate temporal order within the response.

Pair 1 — Literal: "Is class over?"

Q prosody: Calm, neutral rising intonation on sentence-final particle "吗" — genuine question.
R phrases: "当然没有下课" (Of course not) → "不要说话" (Stop talking) → "好好听课" (Pay attention)
SR: F Factual → W Wisdom → W Wisdom

Pair 2 — Deep: "Can he dance?"

Q prosody: Skeptical tone with emphasis on "会" (can) — implies doubt about the person's ability.
R phrases: "他可能会跳舞吧" (Maybe he can) → "但是重在参与" (But what matters is participation)
SR: A Against → V Vague

Pair 3 — Deep: "Is class over?" (same text as Pair 1, different prosody)

Q prosody: Sharper, impatient falling contour — the speaker knows the answer is "no" and is expressing dissatisfaction. Compare with the calm rising tone of Pair 1.
R phrases: "马上就下课了" (It'll be over soon)
SR: W Wisdom + D Disagreement

Pair 4 — Deep: "Does he have anything to do with this?"

Q prosody: Rhetorical tone implying the answer is obviously "no" — the speaker uses a dismissive falling contour.
R phrases: "他跟这件事儿没关系啊" (He has nothing to do with it)
SR: F Factual + R Resonance
