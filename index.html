Abstract

Existing multimodal speech-language understanding methods mainly emphasize cross-modal consistency fusion and are effective at modeling the collaborative relationship between text and speech. However, they remain insufficient for deep pragmatic phenomena such as rhetorical questions, sarcasm, and indirect expressions. In spoken communication, speaker intention is often jointly determined by patterns of consistency and conflict between textual semantics and vocal expression.

To address this issue, we propose the Consistency-Discrepancy Decoupling Network (CDD-Net), which explicitly models cross-modal consistency and semantic discrepancy. TLDL localizes conflict regions at the granularity of text tokens and speech frames, while DSCL separates multimodal evidence into consistency and discrepancy semantic spaces.

Furthermore, DGCP and CDR enhance the discriminability and completeness of discrepancy-aware representations, and the RFR path improves robustness and generalization. Experimental results show that CDD-Net outperforms strong multimodal baselines and advanced cross-modal models on the constructed benchmark.

Model Architecture

CDD-Net serves two complementary tasks on a shared TLDL backbone while keeping task-specific fusion heads.

Response Intention Annotation System

The SR task uses a 14-class taxonomy across five meta-categories, and the sample cards above expose a subset of those labels.

Each prosodic phrase in a response utterance is independently annotated. The labels shown in this demo, such as F, W, A, V, D, and R, are direct examples from the broader SR annotation system.
